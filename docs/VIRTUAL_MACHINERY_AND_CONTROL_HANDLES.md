# Virtual machinery and control handles

## Status

Working research program.  Not a theory of consciousness, not a claim that mental
images are literal internal objects, and not evidence for free will.

The motivating case is abacus expertise:

```text
physical abacus + hands + vision
        -> prolonged training
        -> imagined abacus + overt finger movements
        -> imagined abacus without overt finger movements
```

A learned external interaction loop can become sufficiently internalized that part of
the same task can later run when the external object is absent.

Call that, provisionally, **virtual machinery**.

The useful question is not whether the brain contains a tiny physical abacus.  It is:

> **How much of a learned perception-action machine can be reinstated and used when
> one or more of its original external components are absent?**

---

## 1. The abacus is unusually clean

Abacus-based mental calculation (AMC) is learned in stages.  Trainees first manipulate
a physical abacus, then manipulate an imagined abacus with supporting finger motions,
and eventually can manipulate the imagined device without overt finger movement.

Neuroimaging work places substantial AMC involvement in visuospatial / parietal and
premotor circuitry rather than reducing the skill to ordinary verbal arithmetic.

Useful primary anchors:

- Ku et al. 2012, PLOS ONE, DOI `10.1371/journal.pone.0036410` — sequential
  visuospatial then visuomotor processing during mental-abacus calculation.
- Tanaka et al. / related lesion case, 2012, *Frontiers in Psychology*,
  PMCID `PMC3428809` — a skilled abacus user temporarily lost the mental-abacus
  strategy after a right-hemisphere stroke involving dorsal premotor / inferior
  parietal regions and recovered it later.
- Long-term CATP data have now been released as a multimodal data resource: Mu et al.,
  *Scientific Data* 2026, DOI `10.1038/s41597-025-06500-9` (access requires the
  repository data-use process).

The lesion case is methodologically important.  The virtual learned tool can apparently
be selectively disrupted while the person remains present and can use compensatory
strategies.

So:

```text
subject
    !=
mental abacus
```

The abacus is a callable learned machine, not an acceptable location for the subject.

---

## 2. "As if present" needs an operational definition

Do not accept introspective similarity alone.

Evidence that an absent object/action is partly reinstated can come from several
stronger tests:

```text
cross-condition decoding
    train a representation on physical execution/perception
    test it on imagery/recall (and vice versa)

representational alignment
    category/action contrast vectors point in similar directions

interference
    perturbing the physical/perceptual machinery selectively impairs the imagined use

causal lesion/stimulation
    damage or perturbation to a candidate circuit removes the virtual capability

behavioral geometry
    timing/error structure of the imagined operation preserves structure from the
    physical operation
```

A single activation map saying "the same area lights up" is weak evidence.

## 3. The general phenomenon is not unique to abacus

### Motor imagery

Executed and imagined movement share portions of premotor, parietal, SMA and motor
networks while retaining important differences.

A useful multivariate example is Zabicki et al./related motor-imagery work showing
shared as well as condition-specific networks; see PMCID `PMC3771114`, DOI
`10.3389/fnhum.2013.00564`.

### Visual imagery

Perception and imagery can share content-sensitive representations.  Cichy, Heinzle &
Haynes (2012), DOI `10.1093/cercor/bhr106`, showed cross-decoding of imagined and seen
object/location information.  Earlier work also found perception-like content in early
visual cortex during internally generated imagery.

### Tool incorporation

Iriki, Tanaka & Iwamura (1996), DOI `10.1097/00001756-199610020-00010`, trained macaques
to retrieve objects with a rake.  During tool use, some bimodal parietal receptive
fields expanded to include the rake or the enlarged reachable space.

This is particularly relevant to `PresentMoment`: learning can alter the effective
boundary of the currently actionable body/world system.

## 4. A stronger formulation: learned counterfactual loops

"Virtual machinery" can be stated without metaphysics.

Suppose a practiced physical task normally closes a loop:

```text
state of world/tool
      -> sensory input
      -> internal state
      -> motor command
      -> new world/tool state
      -> sensory input
      -> ...
```

With training, the organism may acquire internal dynamics that predict or reconstruct
parts of the missing loop:

```text
remembered / inferred state
      -> internal prediction
      -> virtual action update
      -> predicted next state
      -> ...
```

The external loop and internal loop need not be identical.  The claim worth testing is
only that the internal loop preserves enough task-relevant geometry to remain useful.

This gives a clean spectrum:

```text
fully external tool
hybrid: imagined tool + overt gesture
mostly internal: imagined tool + covert motor update
fully symbolic/verbal shortcut
```

Expertise may move tasks along this spectrum, and different people may solve the same
problem at different locations on it.

## 5. Control handles: how tiny control can move a huge organism

This now gives a possible answer to the *engineering* half of the subject/control
question.

The high-level control surface need not specify muscle activations, spinal patterns,
cardiorespiratory changes, visual flow, balance corrections, route memory, reward
prediction, etc.

It can operate on learned **handles**:

```text
stand up
reach
say the sentence
play the song
go home
go for a bike ride
reconstruct the abacus
think about person X
```

Each handle can launch a much larger learned dynamical program.

This resembles action chunking, but the important claim is broader: learning can
compress many low-level degrees of freedom into a smaller set of callable control
options.

Human motor-sequence work supports real neural chunking rather than this being only a
software analogy.  Wymbs et al. (Neuron 2012, DOI `10.1016/j.neuron.2012.03.038`)
found increasing motor chunk concatenation with training and linked it to sensorimotor
putamen activity, while frontoparietal activity tracked segmentation.  Basal-ganglia
lesions can impair the organization of learned movement sequences into chunks
(DOI `10.1016/j.nlm.2009.02.009`).

Do not equate basal ganglia with the chooser.  The narrower point is:

> **training can turn a long sequence into a more unitary callable behavioral object.**

That is one biologically plausible route by which a small present control surface can
have leverage over a huge machine.

## 6. The control surface may therefore be learned, not fixed

This changes the "degrees of freedom" question.

An untrained organism may nominally possess thousands of motor possibilities but only
a few useful high-level actions.

After learning, a new effective degree of freedom can appear:

```text
before learning:
    individual low-level operations, difficult to coordinate

after learning:
    one new reliable macro / policy / internal machine that can be launched
```

The physical brain did not gain a new limb.

The **reachable-future geometry** changed because a new reliable trajectory became
available from a compact cue/state.

This is a candidate operational meaning for an acquired control degree of freedom.

It also makes Claude's earlier effective-DOF proposal concrete:

1. enumerate currently available high-level actions/handles;
2. roll the system forward under each;
3. cluster materially distinct future states;
4. count/rank the distinct reachable futures;
5. ask how training, neuromodulation, fatigue, lesion or context changes that rank.

## 7. People can be virtual machinery too — but use careful language

The idea can expand beyond tools, but "a person lives in your brain" is too strong.

A safer statement is:

> experience with another person can build internal predictive/relational models that
> can later influence perception, valuation, memory and choice when that person is
> absent.

Recent hippocampal work shows that social knowledge is encoded relationally and can be
organized in map-like geometry.  For example, Rodríguez Aramendía, Esposito & Kaplan
(PLoS Biology 2025, DOI `10.1371/journal.pbio.3003050`) found hippocampal-formation and
dmPFC representations of social knowledge anchored relative to self-knowledge.

This makes an absent person potentially part of the *current computational world*
without making the absent person physically present.

A remembered person can therefore function as:

```text
a prediction source
an action constraint
a value modifier
a retrieval cue
a simulated conversational partner
a route into another learned organism state
```

That is testable in principle.  It is also very different from claiming that the
simulation is the actual person.

## 8. Relation to the subject question

The two questions remain separate:

```text
A. Why is there a subject at all?

B. How does a subject/system obtain small degrees of control over a huge organism?
```

Virtual machinery helps much more with B than A.

A tentative decomposition is:

```text
maintained subject / conscious regime
             |
       current control-potent surface
             |
        learned handles
       /      |       \
  skill    memory    virtual tool/person model
       \      |       /
       large distributed machinery
             |
        changed future state
```

The subject need not contain the implementation details of the thing it controls.

The subject also need not be identical to any one handle.  A handle can disappear
(mental abacus, language, a particular memory route) while the subject remains.

This is consistent with `SELF_BY_SUBTRACTION.md` and
`PUBLIC_AND_PRIVATE_SUBSPACES.md`.

## 9. First external gate: EEGMMIDB

`experiments/eegmmidb_virtual_machinery.py` tests a deliberately small implication
using the public PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Database (DOI `10.13026/C28G6P`).

For each subject:

```text
execution runs: 3, 7, 11   left vs right fist
imagery runs:   4, 8, 12   left vs right fist
```

The script extracts mu/beta relative band-power geometry and measures:

```text
within execution decoding
within imagery decoding
execution -> imagery transfer
imagery -> execution transfer
left/right contrast-vector cosine alignment
```

### Critical confound

The task uses a left/right visual target to specify left/right hand.

Therefore above-chance cross-decoding can be produced by shared visual/task structure
without motor reinstatement.

The experiment consequently compares:

```text
sensorimotor electrodes
        versus
occipital electrodes
```

The strong story is blocked unless sensorimotor cross-condition transfer exceeds the
occipital control at the group level.

This is still only an instrument/proof-of-contact test, not evidence for subjectivity.

## 10. Kill conditions

Kill or weaken the virtual-machinery story if:

1. cross-condition decoding disappears under cue-matched controls;
2. imagery/execution similarity is no greater in task-relevant circuitry than in
   sensory/confound controls;
3. apparent similarity is explained by global arousal or timing alone;
4. learned chunks do not produce measurable compression of control/reachable-future
   structure;
5. a simpler representational account predicts behavior without any internal rollout;
6. social "simulation" effects reduce to semantic association with no evidence of
   predictive/trajectory structure.

Most importantly:

> **Do not let "as if" turn into ontology.**

The useful claim is functional: a learned internal process can sometimes stand in for
missing parts of an external perception-action loop.

---

## 11. Current working sentence

> **Learning can turn parts of the world into callable internal machinery.  Those
> learned machines may give a small current control surface leverage over a much larger
> organism by compressing complex perception-action trajectories into a few effective
> handles.**

That may explain part of *how control gets small*.

It does not yet explain why any of it is experienced by a subject.
