# Primary-paper map

This file is intentionally biased toward **primary experiments**. Reviews and broad theories can be useful later, but the mechanism should first be anchored to things somebody actually perturbed or measured.

## 1. Acute stress has multiple physiological latencies

**Richter SD, Schürmeyer TH, Schedlowski M, et al. (1996). _Time kinetics of the endocrine response to acute psychological stress._ J Clin Endocrinol Metab. PMID 8626864.**

First-time tandem parachutists were sampled before and after the jump. Heart rate and epinephrine rose during the jump; norepinephrine, cortisol, GH, prolactin and TSH peaked with roughly 10–20 minute latency; cortisol and TSH remained elevated one hour later.

**Why it matters here:** one perturbation leaves a multiscale physiological trajectory. This is the cleanest empirical motivation for `somatic ringdown`.

---

## 2. Hippocampus is part of endocrine stress feedback

**Sapolsky RM, Krey LC, McEwen BS. (1984). _Glucocorticoid-sensitive hippocampal neurons are involved in terminating the adrenocortical stress response._ PNAS. PMID 6592609.**

Evidence that hippocampal glucocorticoid-sensitive neurons participate in feedback control of the adrenocortical stress response.

**Sapolsky RM, Zola-Morgan S, Squire LR. (1991). _Inhibition of glucocorticoid secretion by the hippocampal formation in the primate._ J Neurosci. PMID 1744687.**

Lesions involving hippocampal formation produced glucocorticoid hypersecretion in nonhuman primates.

**Why it matters here:** the hippocampus is not isolated from body regulation; it participates in a slow endocrine feedback architecture while also supporting memory-related functions.

---

## 3. Peripheral arousal can alter central norepinephrine through body-to-brain pathways

**Williams CL, Men D, Clayton EC, Gold PE. (1998). _Norepinephrine release in the amygdala after systemic injection of epinephrine or escapable footshock: contribution of the nucleus of the solitary tract._ Behav Neurosci. PMID 9926823.**

Systemic epinephrine and footshock elevated amygdala norepinephrine; the experiments implicate the NTS in this route.

**Miyashita T, Williams CL. (2003). _Enhancement of noradrenergic neurotransmission in the nucleus of the solitary tract modulates memory storage processes._ Brain Res. PMID 14499960.**

NTS noradrenergic manipulation altered memory storage.

**Hassert DL, Miyashita T, Williams CL. (2004). _The effects of peripheral vagal nerve stimulation at a memory-modulating intensity on norepinephrine output in the basolateral amygdala._ Behav Neurosci. PMID 14979784.**

Peripheral VNS at a memory-modulating intensity increased basolateral-amygdala norepinephrine output.

**Why it matters here:** a peripheral physiological event can re-enter the brain through neural interoceptive pathways and alter memory-relevant neuromodulation.

---

## 4. Heartbeat phase changes threat processing

**Garfinkel SN, Minati L, Gray MA, et al. (2014). _Fear from the heart: sensitivity to fear stimuli depends on individual heartbeats._ J Neurosci. PMID 24806682.**

Fearful faces presented at systole were detected more readily and rated as more intense than at diastole, with stronger amygdala responses.

**Garfinkel SN, Gould van Praag CD, Engels M, et al. (2020). _Interoceptive cardiac signals selectively enhance fear memories._ J Exp Psychol Gen. PMID 33180532.**

Cardiac afferent timing during conditioned fear influenced learning and later memory.

**Why it matters here:** external information arriving at the same nominal `now` is processed differently depending on the body's internal phase.

---

## 5. Respiratory phase participates in sensory-cognitive timing

**Johannknecht M, Kayser C. (2022). _The influence of the respiratory cycle on reaction times in sensory-cognitive paradigms._ Sci Rep. PMID 35173204.**

Across multiple tasks, reaction times covaried with respiratory state and participants tended to align respiration to task events.

**Matsumoto et al. (2023). _Effects of respiratory phases on the processing of emotional and non-emotional visual stimuli._ Psychophysiology. PMID 36715139.**

Fear discrimination was more accurate during inhalation than exhalation, while a non-emotional contrast task did not show the same pattern.

**Della Penna F, et al. (2026). _Breathing Strategies to Influence Perception: Evidence for Interoceptive and Exteroceptive Active Sensing._ J Neurosci. PMID 42045067.**

Participants systematically adjusted breathing relative to anticipated interoceptive and tactile stimuli; performance varied with respiratory phase.

**Why it matters here:** respiration is both a body loop and a temporal coordinate coupled to perception/action.

---

## 6. Arousal / locus coeruleus activity can segment experience

**Clewett D, Huang R, Davachi L. (2025). _Locus coeruleus activation 'resets' hippocampal event representations and separates adjacent memories._ Neuron. PMID 40482639.**

Event boundaries produced pupil-linked arousal and LC-related responses that predicted later memory separation; boundaries also promoted temporal pattern separation in hippocampal dentate gyrus.

**Why it matters here:** danger/salience may alter the organization of the present through gain and segmentation rather than merely stretching a uniform time window.

---

## 7. Modern circuit work makes visceral feedback extremely concrete

**Scott KA, Tan Y, Johnson DN, et al. (2025). _Mechanosensation of the heart and gut elicits hypometabolism and vigilance in mice._ Nature Metabolism. DOI 10.1038/s42255-024-01205-6.**

The authors identified vagal sensory neurons innervating cardiovascular and gastrointestinal tissues. Activating these neurons altered cardiac output, body temperature, energy expenditure, HPA-related brain activity and vigilance-related behavior.

**Why it matters here:** specific body mechanics can be sensed by identified visceral neurons and causally change central state and whole-organism behavior.

**Huerta TS, Chen AC, Chaudhry S, et al. (2025). _Neural representation of cytokines by vagal sensory neurons._ Nature Communications 16:3840. DOI 10.1038/s41467-025-59248-6.**

In vivo calcium imaging showed vagal sensory neurons with distinct real-time responses to inflammatory cytokines.

**Why it matters here:** the body's currently active immune state can also be represented neurally at the first body-to-brain sensory station.

---

# What these papers do NOT establish

They do not jointly prove that there is a single biological object called a `wide present`.

They establish pieces:

```text
heterogeneous physiological time courses
+ body-to-brain sensory return
+ central neuromodulatory consequences
+ heartbeat / respiration phase effects
+ endocrine feedback involving memory-related structures
+ arousal-dependent event segmentation
```

`PresentMoment` is the hypothesis that some of these pieces can be understood together as a **distributed temporal state used by ongoing cognition**.

That synthesis must earn itself experimentally.
